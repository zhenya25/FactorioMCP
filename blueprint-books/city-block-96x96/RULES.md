# City Block 96x96 - design rules

Root book label: `SpaceAge City Block 96x96`.

1. **Grid.** Square blocks 96x96 on an absolute grid. Intersections sit at multiples of 96, so rails run on chunk borders and a block is 3x3 chunks.
2. **Rails.** Double track, right-hand traffic, tracks 3 tiles either side of the block border. Trains: 1 locomotive + 4 wagons.
3. **Snapping.** Every blueprint snaps to the absolute 96x96 grid, so it cannot be misplaced far from the base.
4. **Power trunk.** Big poles in the rail median, part of the intersection blueprint; every block borders it on all four sides.
5. **Block frame.** Every block reserves four slots at its quarter points: roboport at 24/72 tiles from the rail centre lines, big pole on its corner-side diagonal at 21/75 (`00-base/block-frame`). One drone network across all blocks, every tile covered. Fillings must leave these slots free.
6. **Fillings.** One block = one product, shipped by train. Modest throughput; the user scales by stamping another block. Usable interior is about 86x86, corners are cut about 13 tiles by rail turns.
7. **Exempt:** books 1-3 (before rails) follow none of the grid rules.
8. **Planets.** City blocks apply to Nauvis (and possibly Vulcanus). Fulgora, Gleba and Aquilo get their own layouts.
9. **Base book (`0. Основа блока`).** Ground preparation and per-block infrastructure that is not a product: landfill, paving, block frame. A block cell runs from rail centre line to rail centre line; the road strip is the 6 tiles next to each centre line, the rest (84x84) is the block area. A lights-and-radar overlay for the rail median was removed at the user's request: it left unpowered lamps on edge cells.
10. **Trains.** Shared trains with 2.0 interrupts instead of one train per product; station names and schedules follow from that. (Decided 2026-10-05.)
11. **Inside a block.** Belts from station to machines in bulk blocks (smelting, circuits); drones with requester chests in the mall and low-volume blocks.
12. **Generations.** One early variant per product; later tiers come from upgrade planners kept in the base book, not from second blueprints.
13. **Edge cells.** `04-grid` has the 4-way intersection plus T-junction (no north arm), corner (south-east) and straight (west-east); the player rotates them with R. `build_cell(cx, cy, arms)` builds any of them.
14. **Fluids.** Water by pipe (blocks that need it sit by the shore). Crude oil by pipe early, by tanker from distant fields. Sulfuric acid by tanker. Oil products are refined inside the oil block; solids leave it. Tanker trains are 1+4 on the same stations but a separate train group.
15. **Defence.** The user plays with biters. A wall cell is centred on a grid node, like an intersection cell, and the wall stands on the grid line in the centre of the cell - never on a block edge (the user rejected that twice). From outside: maze of loose wall lines with staggered gaps, main wall two thick, gun turrets with the ammo belt, lasers, flamethrowers with the fuel pipe. A rail line leaving the city crosses a wall cell through its centre with gates on the rails (entrance variant). Corners use a flamethrower at 45 degrees. Their reference: `City Block/Defense` in the second quick bar row.
16. **Wall supply post.** Not part of the wall: a block in the first full ring behind it (a half-block of free land lies between), so it takes no hits and supply scales by adding posts. It has a train station; ammo goes onto a belt and flamethrower fuel from tankers into tanks and a pipe, both crossing into the wall cell and running through every wall cell at fixed offsets so cells chain. The station calls a train only below a minimum stock and refills with a reserve for several attack waves. Supply has one direction along the wall (clockwise around the base) so a post feeds the cells downstream and never pushes into the next post. Every wall cell carries a pump on the fuel pipe; the user removes the ones they do not need, so post spacing is free.
17. **Artillery.** Not available early. Every wall cell keeps a free slot for it; a separate add-on blueprint places the artillery there, with shells delivered by drones.
18. **Stations.** A station is a spine: a siding from the block's top track to its bottom track at column 33, 47, 61 or 77. Unloading takes from both sides of the wagons and merges the two half-belts into one full belt (an inserter only fills the far lane - the user rejected a one-sided unloader for this). Loading splits the incoming belt 1 to 4, one belt and six capped chests per wagon, so wagons fill evenly. Stops: `Погрузка` for every loader, `[item=X] Разгрузка` for unloaders, `Депо` for idle trains; train limit 1; open by chest contents.
19. **Block layout.** Input A unloads at spine 33 and leaves it southward, optional input B at spine 77 leaves northward and comes round to row 78; production sits between spines 33 and 61 and flows north; the product crosses on row 37 into the loader on the east side of spine 61. Test a block by stocking its unloading chests, never by feeding belts directly.
